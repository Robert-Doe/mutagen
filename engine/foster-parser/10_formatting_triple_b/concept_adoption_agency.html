The AdoptionAgency Algorithm

The parser's fix for misnested formatting elements — <b><i>x</b>y</i>-shaped input. A much bigger algorithm than this course builds; only its last step ever touches foster parenting.

Two wristbands, put on in one order, someone tries to take them off in the other order. The adoption agency algorithm is the process for sorting that out — finding where the tangle happened, cutting the right band, and re-issuing the pieces in a shape that makes sense. It is not the same process as security walking an unexpected arrival to the gate (foster parenting) — it's a much more involved untangling, of which relocation is only the final step.

What it's for: an end tag (like </b>) whose matching start tag is buried under other, still-open elements, rather than being the current node.

What this course builds: nothing of the algorithm itself, through Module 13 — it was out of scope for the original 00–13 roadmap. That boundary has since been crossed: Module 14 builds and verifies the full algorithm, added to the course specifically to cover it, including a dedicated treatment of how it fits into "how the parser handles malformed HTML" generally. This page now stays as the vocabulary-web term-disambiguation entry — start at Module 14 for the real mechanism.

The one point of contact with this course's subject: the algorithm's final relocation step inserts its result at "appropriate place for inserting a node" (Prerequisite P5) — the exact same algorithm foster parenting lives inside — using a computed common ancestor as an override target. If that override target happens to be table-family and the flag is live, the relocation fosters. Henri Sivonen's 2008 observation (see REFERENCES.md): on inspection, this isn't a special case at all — it's foster parenting as usual, just reached from a different algorithm. Module 14's own Case 3 (a <table> misnested inside a <b>) verifies exactly this composition against a real browser.

The confused pair

"Adoption agency" and "foster parenting" share adoption/parenting-family vocabulary that invites conflation, and both really do relocate nodes — but one is a large, general misnesting-repair algorithm that occasionally happens to end in a foster-parenting-shaped move, and the other is a tiny, specific two-condition gate. Treating them as synonyms, or as two names for the same mechanism, is the single most common mix-up this vocabulary web exists to head off.

How this connects

Reaches foster parenting only through its last step's override target. Shares no mechanism with reconstruction — they're siblings under the active-formatting-elements list, not the same operation (reconstruction re-creates missing elements; adoption agency untangles misnested ones). Shares nothing but a naming collision with DOM reparenting.
