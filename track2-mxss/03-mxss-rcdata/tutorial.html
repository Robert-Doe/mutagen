Rawtext Wrapper Removalthe mutation everyone expects

The intuitive mXSS: a sanitizer deletes a <title> or <xmp> it doesn't like, and its inert text content springs to life on re-parse. This module proves that a correct serializer stops it — and shows the three ways that proof falls apart.

What this module proves

Text promoted out of a raw-text element is escaped by the serializer, so the naive rawtext mXSS is closed. It re-opens if (1) the filter is string-based, (2) the wrapper is kept, or (3) a namespace / scripting flag / template is in play — Modules 4, 5, 7.

01

The Big Analogy

A sealed drum arrives at Kofi's shed labelled <xmp> engine oil. The rule for a sealed oil drum: don't open it, don't sort it, just log the label and pass it. Inside, though, is a smaller box marked contraband. As long as the drum stays sealed, the box is going nowhere.

Now the next officer decides the shed isn't licensed for oil and removes the drum — tips the contents onto the sorting table. Here is the thing the attacker was counting on and the thing that actually saves the day: when Kofi writes the contents onto the open manifest, he is required to quote every field. The box marked contraband gets written as the literal text "contraband" — a label, not a command. The next officer reads a crate named "contraband", shrugs, and moves on. The drum's seal being broken didn't help the smuggler, because the open-manifest quoting rule caught what the sealed-drum rule would have hidden.

Unless… the drum is kept (still sealed, still not quoted). Or the clerk doing the check just runs a text search over the paperwork instead of opening anything. Or the drum was in the fishing co-op's hall, where the rules are different. Those are Modules 5, "the regex", and 4/7.

02

What Happens, Step by Step

  1. Attacker submits <title><img src=x onerror=alert(1)></title>.
  2. The sanitizer parses it (scripting off). <title> is RCDATA, so its content is one text node: "<img src=x onerror=alert(1)>". No element, no handler.
  3. <title> isn't on the allow-list. The sanitizer removes it and keeps its children — promoting that text node to be a direct child of the fragment.
  4. The sanitizer serializes. The text node's parent is now the fragment root, not <title>, so the serializer escapes it: &lt;img src=x onerror=alert(1)&gt;.
  5. The browser re-parses that at innerHTML. &lt; is a character reference for < — it decodes to a literal < character in a text node, not a tag. Inert.

Step 4 is the save. Verified for style script xmp iframe noembed noframes title textarea against Chrome 148: the promoted text always comes out escaped.

The Code, Explained

The DOM sanitizer's "remove but keep children" branch, and the serializer step that saves it:

// sanitizer: element not allowed → drop it, promote its cleaned children
if (!allowed) {
  clean(child);
  for (const g of childrenOf(child)) { g.parent = node; out.push(g); }
  continue;
}

// serializer: a text node under a NORMAL parent is escaped
out += NO_ESCAPE_TEXT.has(parentName) ? child.data : escapeText(child.data);
//                    ▲ style/script/xmp/...        ▲ < > & → &lt; &gt; &amp;

And the string filter that has none of this protection:

regexSanitizer('<img/onerror=alert(1)//src=x>')
  // the rule is  /\son\w+\s*=/  — it needs whitespace before "on".
  // "/onerror" has a slash. Nothing is stripped. Payload ships intact.

Limits — What You Cannot Do Yet

Can't do yet

  • Actually fire an alert through a correct sanitizer using rawtext alone — you can't, and that's the finding
  • Exploit a kept <style> — needs a namespace (M5) or template (M7) to change what </style> means on reparse

Can do

  • Prove the naive rawtext mXSS is closed, for every raw-text element, against Chrome 148
  • Name the single serializer step (escapeText on promoted text) the whole result depends on
  • Bypass a realistic string sanitizer two different ways

07

Brain Exercise

The sanitizer removes <title> and keeps its text, which the serializer escapes. What if the sanitizer instead keeps <title> (it's on a permissive allow-list) with text content </title><img src=x onerror=alert(1)>? Does the escape still save you?

Yes, still saved — because <title> is RCDATA, not raw text. The serializer's no-escape list is style script xmp iframe noembed noframes noscript plaintext. <title> and <textarea> are not on it — their text content is escaped on serialize. So a kept <title> ships as <title>&lt;/title&gt;&lt;img…&gt;</title> and stays inert. The genuinely dangerous "kept wrapper" is <style> or <xmp> — those emit raw — and even then you need a context change on reparse for the </style> to bite. That's Module 5.

08

There Are No Dumb Questions

Q: So Heiderich's 2013 paper was wrong about rawtext mXSS?
A: No — it was right for 2013. Old IE's innerHTML serializer did not reliably escape, and lots of sanitizers were string-based. The class was real and widespread. Browser serializers were hardened over the following decade specifically to close it. The mechanism the paper named is still exactly what Modules 4–7 exploit; only the plain rawtext-only version got fixed.
Q: Why does <style> get raw serialization when <title> doesn't?
A: <style> holds CSS, which is full of > (child selectors) and & (nesting) — escaping it would corrupt the stylesheet. <title> holds a plain string; escaping it is harmless. The spec draws the line by usefulness, and that line is the attack surface.
Q: Is the "regex sanitizer" a straw man?
A: Regex-only HTML sanitizers still ship in the wild — in template engines, in "quick fixes", in languages without a good DOM library. The bypasses shown here (/ as a separator, entity-encoded schemes) are decades old and still work. The point of the module is the contrast: the same two payloads that walk straight past the regex are stopped cold by the tree walk.
Q: <plaintext> is in the no-escape list — is it a vector?
A: <plaintext> has no end tag — once opened it eats the rest of the input. A sanitizer can't "remove the wrapper and promote children" because everything after it is the children, as one text node. Weird, but not a mutation vector. It mostly shows up as a denial-of-content bug.
